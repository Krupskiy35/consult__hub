CCIPU Ukraine Desk priority sectors: where can Ukraine and Italy create joint business opportunities?

Ukraine’s recovery brings major challenges—and concrete business opportunities.

Following a series of meetings in Ukraine and Italy—with representatives of both parliaments, Italian institutions and businesses—we have identified where Ukraine’s needs align with Italy’s strengths. Seven areas stand out. Below, we examine the seven sectors with the greatest practical potential for Ukrainian–Italian cooperation.

1. Water supply and wastewater management

Russian attacks continue to damage water infrastructure, particularly in regions near the front line. At the same time, many communities farther from the fighting need to modernise ageing water networks and treatment facilities.

Italy has substantial technological and operational expertise in this field. We see two avenues for cooperation. The first is mobile and containerised water-treatment systems for frontline areas and critical infrastructure. The second is the participation of Italian companies in rebuilding municipal water supply and wastewater systems.

Cooperation may range from equipment supply and works commissioned by municipalities to comprehensive upgrades of water utilities, potentially involving private operators through public–private partnerships.

2. Energy and energy resilience

Ukrainian businesses and communities need solutions that keep essential operations running even when the power system is damaged. The most promising areas are distributed generation, energy efficiency and technologies that protect critical infrastructure.

Italy is described here as the world’s largest renewable-energy operator, with 63.3 GW of capacity. This experience is directly relevant to Ukraine’s needs. We are working to engage Italian technology partners capable of bringing these solutions to the Ukrainian market.

3. Industry and construction

The recovery of Ukrainian industry is driving demand for modern equipment, engineering solutions, construction materials and manufacturing technologies. Italy is Europe’s second-largest industrial economy, and machinery is its leading export sector.

A further opportunity is to establish manufacturing operations in Ukraine together with Italian producers. There is also interest in Italian finishing materials and modern structural components that, in some projects, could replace conventional steel structures.

4. Logistics and warehousing infrastructure

Russian attacks have destroyed a significant number of Ukraine’s large logistics facilities. Rebuilding sites of 50,000–100,000 m² takes time, is complex and may not always be appropriate given the security risks.

Italian companies offer an alternative: rapidly deployable modular warehouses of 1,500, 3,000 and 5,000 m². Modularity, geographic distribution and scalability make it possible to restore lost capacity quickly. SIMEST and SACE have already indicated their willingness in principle to explore financing for such projects. We therefore see this as one of the areas with the fastest potential for implementation.

5. Agricultural processing and industrial equipment

Ukraine is an important grain supplier, including to Italy’s animal-feed industry. However, high maritime transport costs are making the shipment of large volumes of raw materials increasingly inefficient.

One solution is to move part of the processing activity to Ukraine, involving Italian equipment manufacturers in joint ventures for primary and advanced processing. Italy has considerable experience in turning agricultural products into high-value brands: its agri-food exports have exceeded €70 billion.

For Ukrainian businesses, this means new production facilities, jobs and broader export opportunities. For Italian partners, it offers lower logistics costs and stable manufacturing partnerships.

6. Waste management and the circular economy

Italy recycles 91.6% of waste—twice the EU average, according to the figures cited in this article. It has experience in developing facilities for sorting, recycling and energy recovery from municipal waste. In Ukraine, such projects could address environmental challenges while strengthening regional energy resilience.

Another opportunity is the recycling of construction and demolition waste, volumes of which are increasing as a result of destruction. Italian companies have substantial expertise in this area, as much of Italy’s construction activity involves refurbishment and renovation.

Ukraine will, of course, need an appropriate regulatory framework. We have already discussed this with a parliamentary group in Ukraine’s Verkhovna Rada and with representatives of the Italian Parliament, and received expressions of willingness to cooperate. We intend to work on adapting relevant Italian legislative experience.

7. Bilateral trade

Italy is already one of the key markets for Ukrainian exports. The next step is to develop direct commercial relationships between companies. For Ukrainian manufacturers, this means buyers, distributors and partners in Italy; for Italian companies, it means customers and distribution channels in Ukraine.

We support Ukrainian companies’ participation in Italian trade fairs and business events. We are also exploring the idea of a major exhibition of Italian products and technologies in Ukraine.
